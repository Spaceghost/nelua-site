2026-11-21 UTC · Linux x86-64

The revised substring search was 123.50× faster than previous Nelibc when aaaaz matched at the end of a 64 KiB buffer filled with a bytes and one final z. The GCC/glibc median fell from 166.87 to 1.34 µs per call.

Loop, pointer-call and checksum overhead is included equally, not subtracted. Buffers are hot and aligned; this is not a DRAM bandwidth, cold-cache, multicore or application throughput result. The record parser is a synthetic combined workload; qsort includes initializing its input, and malloc/free includes writing the first and last byte.

What to optimize next

Start with measured losses in frequently used operations, retain the winning cases, and require correctness before promoting a faster path. This ordering reflects this corpus; it is not a claim about every application's CPU profile.

  1. strlen: glibc + Nelibc measured 0.53× baseline speed at 128 bytes. Keep this case in the comparison while testing short, long, aligned and unaligned inputs.
  2. memchr-absent: Cosmopolitan + Nelibc measured 0.55× baseline speed at 8 bytes. Keep this case in the comparison while testing short, long, aligned and unaligned inputs.
  3. strtod-decimal: glibc + Nelibc measured 0.57× baseline speed. Keep this case in the comparison while testing short, long, aligned and unaligned inputs.

Correctness first: investigate or document the newly failing corpus cases before promoting optimizations: musl / Zig: regression/sigprocmask-internal.

Next evidence: application profiles to weight the cases; more CPUs and working-set sizes; allocator contention and I/O workloads. A single-host microbenchmark cannot establish a globally fastest libc.
